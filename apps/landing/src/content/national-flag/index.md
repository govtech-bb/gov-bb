On 30 November 1966, Barbados' national flag rose for the first time at the Garrison Savannah, near Bridgetown. Once a parade ground for British troops, it became the setting for the ceremony marking Barbados' independence from Britain.

## The design of the flag

The flag has three equal vertical bands, with gold between two bands of ultramarine blue. A black trident, a spear with three points, sits in the centre of the gold band.

The flag is three parts wide for every two parts high.

## What the flag represents

The blue reflects the sea and sky of Barbados, while the gold recalls the sand along its shores.

The trident is a symbol of Neptune, the Roman god of the sea. It also appeared on Barbados' former colonial seal, held by Britannia, a figure representing Britain.

The trident's three points remain intact, but the long pole beneath them has been cut short. This break marks Barbados' separation from British rule and its emergence as an independent nation.

The trident's three points represent three principles of democracy: government of the people, government by the people and government for the people.

## Who designed the flag

As Barbados looked towards independence, Grantley W. Prescod, an art teacher at Parkinson Secondary School, created the design that would become the nation's flag.

His design was chosen from 1,029 entries in a national competition. Prescod later served as an Education Officer.

## Flying and using the flag

The national flag must be displayed and used with care. Certain uses, including selling goods bearing the flag or using it for business, require a government licence.
