# Legal review: reg. 3(2) exclusions for the food business licence start page

**Status:** Draft, not published. Held out of
`apps/landing/src/content/apply-environmental-health-food-business-license/index.md`
until legal advisers confirm the plain-English rendering below.

**Source:** Health Services (Food Hygiene) Regulations, 1969 (S.I. 1969/232,
amended by S.I. 1978/111), made under section 10 of the Health Services Act,
Cap. 44. Regulation 3(2), with the definition of "open food" from regulation
2(1).

**Why this needs legal review:** regulation 3(2) is a double-negative
construction with a parenthetical exception embedded mid-sentence. Any plain
-English version has to decide how far that exception reaches, and that decision
changes who needs a licence.

## Verbatim text

Regulation 3(2):

> The expression "food business" does not include any agricultural activity or
> (except so far as the handling of food may be involved in the course of a
> retail business or in the course of supplying food for immediate consumption)
> so much of any trade or business as consists of the handling of food at, in or
> upon-
>
> (a) any dock, wharf, careenage, public warehouse or cold store; or
>
> (b) (except for any requirements relating to the transport and carrying of
> meat) any premises or place occupied by a carrier of goods for the purposes of
> his trade or business as such a carrier; or
>
> (c) any slaughter-house; or
>
> (d) any premises or places occupied by a wholesaler of raw vegetables and used
> exclusively for the purposes of his trade or business as such a wholesaler; or
>
> (e) any premises which are not used for the storage of open food.

Regulation 2(1):

> "open food" means food not contained in containers and so closed as to exclude
> all risk of contamination

## Proposed plain-English version

Proposed heading: **When you do not need this licence**

> You do not need a Food Business Licence for:
>
> - farming and other agricultural activity
> - handling food at a dock, wharf, careenage, public warehouse or cold store
> - handling food at premises used by a goods carrier, though you must still
>   follow the rules for transporting and carrying meat
> - handling food at a slaughterhouse
> - handling food at premises used only by a wholesaler of raw vegetables
> - premises that are not used to store open food
>
> Open food means food that is not in a container closed well enough to keep out
> all risk of contamination.
>
> You still need a licence if you handle food at any of these places as part of
> a retail business, or to supply food that people will eat straight away.

## Questions for legal advisers

1. **Reach of the retail and immediate-consumption exception.** The draft treats
   the parenthetical as qualifying items (a) to (e) but not "any agricultural
   activity", because the parenthetical sits after "or" and attaches to the
   second limb. On that reading a farm gate shop selling produce retail is
   outside the licence. Is that the intended effect?

2. **Whether item (e) swallows the list.** If premises not used to store open
   food are already outside the definition, items (a) to (d) appear to add
   little. Confirm whether (e) is intended to be read that broadly, and whether
   stating it publicly would understate who needs a licence.

3. **Scope of the meat carve-out in (b).** The draft renders it as "you must
   still follow the rules for transporting and carrying meat". Confirm this does
   not imply a carrier needs a full food business licence for meat transport.

4. **"Careenage" and "slaughter-house".** Both are kept as the statutory terms.
   Confirm whether "careenage" should be retained for a public audience or
   explained.

5. **Currency of the exclusions.** Confirm regulation 3(2) has not been amended
   or displaced since S.I. 1978/111, and that no later instrument governs food
   business licensing.

## Related open question

Regulation 27(2) states that a food business licence "shall expire on the 31st
day of December next after the date of issue". Environmental Health advises that
in practice this licence renews on the anniversary of registration, unlike the
other Environmental Health licences, which do expire on 31 December. The MDA has
approved that rule, together with the requirement to apply for renewal at least
30 days before expiry, and the start page follows both.

Neither rule appears in the 1969 regulations, and the anniversary rule departs
from the express wording of regulation 27(2). Confirm whether the divergence is
intended and on what authority it rests, so the page can cite the correct
instrument if one exists.
